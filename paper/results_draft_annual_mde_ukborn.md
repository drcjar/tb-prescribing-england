### Annual panel (UTLAs, annual TB notifications 2017–2024, prescribing 2014–2024)

**Data.** Annual UTLA counts from the UKHSA regional reports closely matched the Fingertips
three-year counts for 2022–24 (149 UTLAs; r = 0.996; total ratio 1.008).

**Primary model** (1,191 area-years, 149 UTLAs; exposure over years *t*−3 to *t*−1). No drug group
was associated with TB incidence. IRRs per 10% within-area increase in prescribing:
- **Immunosuppressive drugs:** oral corticosteroids 0.985 (0.940–1.033); inhaled corticosteroids
  0.968 (0.910–1.030); immunosuppressants 1.010 (0.986–1.035)
- **Other candidate drugs:** PPIs 0.966 (0.913–1.022); statins 1.009 (0.949–1.073); metformin 0.977
  (0.927–1.031)
- **Controls:** levothyroxine (negative control) 0.967 (0.928–1.008); antituberculosis drugs
  (positive control) 1.004 (0.999–1.010)

All q ≥ 0.67.

**Sensitivity and falsification:**
- **Other exposure windows:** results were similar with a one-year lag, with concurrent exposure,
  and after excluding 2020–21.
- **Total prescribing volume:** adjusting for it again produced spurious inverse associations,
  including for the negative control (levothyroxine 0.940, 0.895–0.988).
- **Lead test:** future vitamin D prescribing was associated with past TB (1.026, 1.001–1.052).

### Minimum detectable effects

Table X compares the smallest effects the primary annual panel could detect with 80% power against
the population effects expected from published individual-level relative risks and UK prevalence
of use.

**Oral corticosteroids** (OR 4.9; prevalence 0.9%):
- **Expected effect:** a 10% within-area increase in use would raise TB incidence by 0.34%
  (population attributable fraction 3.4%).
- **Detectable effect:** 7.0%, about 20 times larger.
- **Power:** 3.4% to detect the expected effect. An individual-level relative risk of about 257
  would be needed to reach 80% power.

**Other drug groups:**

Power was below 4% for every group. The rolling three-year panel was similarly underpowered (oral
corticosteroid MDE 7.7%).

### Oral corticosteroids and TB by place of birth (9 regions, 2015–2024)

There were 11,876 UK-born and 36,633 non-UK-born TB notifications in 2015–2024.

**Non-UK-born TB:**
- **Lagged exposures:** OCS prescribing was not associated with TB.
- **Lead test:** it failed. Future OCS items (lead 3: 1.36, 1.03–1.80), future prednisolone
  milligrams (1.26, 1.02–1.54) and future PPI prescribing (1.25, 1.02–1.53) were associated with
  past TB, indicating confounding by regional trends.

**UK-born TB:**
- **OCS items:** positive point estimates, not statistically significant (lag 1 1.26, 0.95–1.68;
  lag 2 1.32, 0.93–1.88; lag 3 1.32, 0.85–2.05).
- **Prednisolone milligrams per resident:** associated with UK-born TB at lags of 2 years (1.37,
  1.08–1.73) and 3 years (1.38, 1.04–1.83). These estimates:
  - were unchanged after excluding 2020–21
  - were not reproduced by lead exposures (0.95–1.18, all non-significant)
  - were not seen with levothyroxine, PPIs or statins
  - did not survive correction for the six steroid tests (Holm-adjusted p = 0.09 and
    0.16; Benjamini–Hochberg q = 0.09 and 0.10).

Across all 90 adjusted regional models, 7 were nominally significant at p < 0.05, against 4.5
expected by chance.

These estimates are implausibly large. Under the model used for the MDE, a 10% increase in the
prevalence of a drug's use can increase incidence by at most 10%, whatever the individual relative
risk. Under published relative risks the expected effect is below 1%. An IRR of 1.37 per 10% is
therefore incompatible with a causal effect of steroid prescribing. It most likely reflects
region-specific trends in UK-born TB (which fell by 40–60% in most regions between 2014 and 2024)
that coincide with the fall in steroid dose, estimated from only nine clusters.
