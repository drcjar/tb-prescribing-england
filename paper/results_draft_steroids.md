### Oral corticosteroid prescribing trends and changes in TB incidence

**Trends, England 2014–2024.** Oral corticosteroid (OCS) items fell from 141.5 to 127.7 per 1,000
residents per year (−9.7%).
- **Substances:** prednisolone accounted for 92% of items in 2014 and fell 12.9%. Dexamethasone
  fell 4.7%. Hydrocortisone, used mainly as replacement therapy, rose 41.9%.
- **Dose:** prednisolone tablet milligrams dispensed per resident fell 21.2% (26.5 to 20.9 g per
  1,000 residents per year), more than items did. Milligrams per item fell from 205 to 186 (−9.2%),
  so courses got smaller as well as fewer.
- **Timing:** most of the fall came in 2020–21, with a partial recovery from 2022
  (`outputs/steroids/ocs_national_trends.png`).

Over the same period England's TB incidence fell to a low in 2020 and then rose.

**National correlation.** Annual OCS items and TB incidence were correlated in levels (r = 0.63,
p = 0.04, 11 years), driven by the shared 2020 dip. Year-on-year changes were not correlated at any
lag from 0 to 3 years (r between −0.54 and 0.23, all p ≥ 0.2).

**Regional annual panel (9 regions, 2015–2024).**
- **Fixed effects only:** OCS prescribing lagged 1–3 years gave inverse point estimates (IRR per 10%
  increase 0.85–0.91), none significant with small-sample cluster-robust inference (t(8)).
- **Adjusted for in-migration and age structure:** estimates were null, e.g. lag 1 0.997 (0.788–1.260),
  lag 3 0.940 (0.801–1.103).
- **Prednisolone milligrams as exposure:** results were similar (lag 1 adjusted 1.078, 0.881–1.318).

**UTLA long differences.** Across 138 UTLAs, the median change in OCS items was −8.2% (2014–16 to
2019–21), and the median change in TB incidence was −21.7% (2014–16 to 2022–24). Changes were not
correlated (Spearman ρ = −0.13, p = 0.12). The adjusted TB ratio per 10% increase in OCS was 1.000
(0.934–1.072) (`outputs/steroids/ocs_tb_long_difference.png`).
